# Phone TXT chapter import to PodJS watch

On 2026-09-25, an isolated physical test exercised the phone companion's production TXT chapter decision and Bluetooth library sync path against a fresh PodJS watch guest. The phone test used `PhoneCompanionRepository.inspectLocalContentImport` to detect a two-heading TXT, `useTxtChapterImport` to select chapter mode, and `confirmLocalContentImport` with an in-memory Room database and owned file store. It did not operate the system file picker or alter the installed phone library.

The test made chapter one 130,000 additional Chinese characters long. The phone stored its text in the production file-backed content store, then `PhoneBluetoothSyncManager` used the paired RFCOMM socket after checking the guest watch's device ID `02fbd372-98ab-447b-b9ac-5004336ec027` and persistent session. Phone instrumentation passed 1/1 and reported two articles sent. The guest watch listener reported three successful actions, one `import-txt-novel` channel and two articles in order, `第一章` then `第二章`; both chapter URLs retained the numbered `/chapter/` identity. Its production `services.getArticle` hydrated the first chapter to 130,009 characters and matched the expected full repeated text, with the second body intact. The guest SQLite snapshot is `.codex-tmp/watch-source-replace-20260925/txt-novel-guest-final.sqlite`: `quick_check=ok`, no foreign-key errors, one channel, two articles, two body caches and one sync peer.

The production `ReaderScreen` was mounted in the guest using the transferred first chapter. A separate OWW242 instrumentation test passed 1/1 and captured the TextureView five seconds after the first visible frame: [TXT chapter reader image](../artifacts/txt-novel-reader-large.png) shows the title and multiple body lines. The device's charging overlay prevented a valid system-wide screenshot; the TextureView capture bypassed it. No synthetic physical input was sent. The TXT chapter fixtures are `tests/runtime-phone-txt-novel-peer`, `tests/android-phone-txt-novel-reader/TxtNovelReaderUiTest.java`, and `Android_WatchRSS_Phone/app/src/androidTest/java/com/lightningstudio/watchrss/phone/connection/bluetooth/PhonePodjsImportedTxtNovelDeviceTest.kt`.

The phone test APK SHA-256 was `b2c02e9a4c4c5bedf1f29142a27b02ff93b20222e13d0984220c533b741830c0`; the watch UI test APK SHA-256 was `b42a14ed8f49b4b53ebce38cbea1be6e1e077d4709f2e43b11b4e9cc259f764f`. The PNG SHA-256 is `cff4351d62821bd674c17e92e81dfca24dd6f49feb21ae719096b372e77f29d2`.

After verification, the owned watch guest and its test package and the phone test package were uninstalled. The main PodJS watch app resumed. Its pre/post fixture snapshots differ at the file level but every SQL table's ordered rows match; the same 16 channels, 6006 articles, 7 memo notes, 105 body caches, 6 saved states and 2 peers remain, and `quick_check` and foreign-key checks pass. The test proves this two-chapter transport and first-page rendering path. It does not prove the phone picker or visible chapter-choice prompt, chapter-list navigation, whole-book scale, physical touch, or final visual parity.

The native watch `FeedViewModel` shows 12 entries initially, adds 8 per page and caps the visible list at 200; the PodJS feed currently uses the same cap. This source-parity limit means scrolling alone cannot reach every chapter of the four imported TXT novels in the migrated library (5,378 chapters total). A 205-chapter SQL fixture confirms that the production search service can find `Chapter 0` beyond the list cap, but the visible search flow still needs real-book acceptance; the two-chapter fixture does not establish it.

The search query now also matches native description, summary and original-body fields and treats `%`, `_` and backslash literally. On a read-only copy of the migrated SQLite database, one sampled chapter at offset 300 within a 2,583-chapter channel was returned by the same title search condition in 0.064 seconds on the development machine. This is one offline query, not watch search latency or visible navigation evidence.
